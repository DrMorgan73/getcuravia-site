Each program has its own consent

Step 1 — Enrollment

Staff enroll the patient in the Suite. The patient agrees on their phone, in their language.

Step 2 — Education first

Short steps with the patient — including one tap that turns on the phone's movement sensor. Only then is the one-time setup logged.

Step 3 — Daily check-ins, sensor-verified

The patient starts a movement session in the app; the sensor confirms real activity while the system times it. Then pain, function, and adherence — and only a sensor-verified day counts.

Step 4 — The monthly tier

16 or more check-in days reaches the full tier. 2–15 days still counts. The system assigns it.

Step 5 — Review and a real call

The team reviews the data, manages the patient, and holds one live conversation. Minutes are logged as they happen.

Step 6 — The risk flag

Climbing pain, silent check-ins, or a sensor that stops seeing movement raise a flag. The patient gets a call this week.

Who qualifies — and what’s in it for you

The patient criteria and the incentive to take part, in the same plain language a patient hears it. RTM is an add-on to care already underway at Boston Pain Center — not a sign-up for new patients.

Patient criteria

A patient joins the program when all of these are true:

  • Already a Boston Pain Center patientRTM is for patients already under care — not new sign-ups off the street.
  • A condition managed with a home programThe first cohort is low back pain patients on a prescribed home exercise program.
  • Has a smartphoneCheck-ins and movement sensing run in the CURAVIA app. No extra device to buy, wear, or charge.
  • Consent, twice overRTM monitoring consent at enrollment, plus a separate movement-sensor consent — activity only, never location.
  • Can check in at least 16 days in the monthThat is the participation level the program is built around — the days set the monthly tier.
  • Setup education completed firstApp orientation, what a check-in day means, cadence, and cost-share — finished with a team member before enrollment counts.
Told up front: Medicare RTM can carry a patient cost-share. Patients hear that during setup education, before they agree — no surprises later.

The incentive to participate

No cash, no gifts. What patients get is their own progress, made visible — and a team that is actually watching between visits.

  • Your streak, day by dayEvery check-in day builds a visible streak in the app — patients see themselves showing up.
  • Your 16-day progress barA simple bar fills toward the full month, so patients always know where they stand.
  • Your pain trend over timeScores plotted across the month — improvement the patient can see, not just feel.
  • A milestone at the 16-day monthRecognition for completing a full month of participation.
  • Care between visitsThe team watches the check-ins, flags problems early, and calls live every month. The patient is never on their own between appointments.
By design: incentives reward participation — doing the check-ins — never outcomes, and they stay nominal. The exact form of the 16-day milestone recognition is held for compliance sign-off before it goes live.

Second program · Runs entirely by phone

Yes — there’s another one: Chronic Care Management (CCM)

RTM is not the only reimbursed program that runs through the phone. CCM is the strongest second program for Boston Pain Center — Medicare pays for care coordination done by phone, with no device, no app data, and no sensor. Just documented clinical-staff time, every month. It is the recommended next build in CURAVIA, on the same board-and-audit-trail pattern as RTM.

RTM — built today

App check-ins + movement sensor

The patient reports data through the CURAVIA app. A day counts only when the phone’s movement sensor has seen real movement.

  • Needs the appDaily check-ins on the patient’s smartphone, 16+ days for the full tier.
  • Best forPatients on a prescribed home program — first cohort: low back pain.
  • Pays$95–$105 per patient per month at the full tier (see codes above).

CCM — recommended next

Who qualifies for CCM

A wider door than RTM — no smartphone or home program required:

  • 2 or more chronic conditionsExpected to last at least 12 months — for example chronic pain alongside hypertension, diabetes, arthritis, or depression.
  • Patient consent on recordExplained up front, including that Medicare CCM can carry a patient cost-share — same honest approach as RTM.
  • A care plan, shared with the patientCreated at enrollment and updated as the phone work happens.
  • 20+ minutes of coordination a monthPhone calls, referral and result follow-up, medication coordination — timed and logged in the Suite, never reconstructed later.
One patient, two programs? RTM and CCM cover different work — app-reported monitoring vs. phone care coordination — and a patient can be in both when each program’s own rules are met and the time is not double-counted. CURAVIA keeps the two boards and timers separate.

CCM is not the only program that runs without the mobile app. CPM, PCM, and TCM also run entirely on documented phone and care-team time in the Suite — no smartphone, no check-in app, and no device required from the patient. TCM — Transitional Care Management

The post-discharge program — episodic, not monthly

TCM pays for the work around a hospital or facility discharge: a phone (or video) contact within 2 business days of discharge, medication reconciliation, and a follow-up visit within 7 or 14 days. It is billed per discharge, not per month — the one program here that is episodic.

Per discharge / episode

Who qualifies

  • Any BPC patient discharged homeFrom a hospital, rehab, or skilled facility stay — no chronic-condition count required.
  • Contact within 2 business daysPhone contact is the trigger — miss the window and the episode cannot be billed. CURAVIA’s discharge board would count the days down.
  • Visit + medication reconciliationVisit within 7 days (99496) or 14 days (99495), meds reconciled by the visit.

What it pays

  • ~$200–$280 per dischargeOne payment per qualifying transition — the fastest-visit tier pays most.
  • 50 discharges / month: ~$10,000–$14,000Roughly $120,000–$168,000 a year at that discharge pace — illustrative; it follows BPC’s actual discharge volume.
  • Stacks on top of the monthly programsA TCM episode covers the discharge window; the patient’s monthly RTM / care-management enrollment continues around it, under each program’s own rules.
How they fit together — read this before adding the columns up: RTM stacks with one care-management program for the same patient, because the work and the time are different and never double-counted. CCM, CPM, and PCM are alternatives for a given patient in a given month — a patient sits in one of them, not all three at once. So the combined revenue story is: RTM + the right care-management program per patient, plus TCM episodes on top whenever a patient is discharged. How CURAVIA is paid

CURAVIA (IEC MSO) is paid a fixed flat fee for each program a patient is enrolled in: RTM $25 · CCM $15 · CPM $15 · PCM $20 per patient per month · TCM $50 per discharge.

Each fee is a fixed flat amount: it was benchmarked once, at signing, to 25% of that program’s expected collections, and then frozen. It is never billed as a percentage of collections and is never tied to referrals — that structure is deliberate, so it stays clear of Massachusetts anti-kickback rules. The clinic keeps 100% of its insurance reimbursement.

When a patient is enrolled in more than one program, the per-program fees add up — but the total CURAVIA fee is capped at $50 per patient per month across all programs combined. TCM is $50 per discharge, flat for both codes. The fees cover CURAVIA running the programs end to end: the care team, the CURAVIA platform, each program’s consent, the compliance gates, and the billing evidence and audit trail behind every claim.
